# Data sources and data-rights policy

This document explains where the project's data comes from, how each source
is treated, and what is — and is not — redistributed in this repository.

> **Not legal advice.** The classifications below are conservative
> engineering decisions made to avoid redistributing data whose reuse terms
> are restrictive or unclear. They are not legal conclusions. Where a
> publisher's terms were reviewed, the link is given; anyone reusing data
> should read the publisher's current terms themselves.

## Code licence versus data rights

| Layer | Licence / terms |
|---|---|
| Source code, tests, configuration schemas, documentation | [MIT](LICENSE) |
| Synthetic demonstration data in `data/demo/` generated by `scripts/generate_synthetic_demo_data.py` | Created by this project; covered by the MIT licence |
| Third-party Python dependencies (`requirements.txt`) | Their own open-source licences |
| Third-party data (statistics, survey results, central-bank text, market prices) | **The publisher's own terms. The MIT licence does not license third-party data.** |

A publicly accessible URL does not by itself grant a right to copy or
redistribute its content.

## Status vocabulary

The machine-readable registry is [`config/data_sources.yaml`](config/data_sources.yaml);
the publication-safety guard (`scripts/check_publication_safety.py`) enforces it.

| Status | Meaning in this repository |
|---|---|
| `permitted_with_attribution` | Publisher terms reviewed; reproduction allowed with source attribution. Values may appear in reviewed public fixtures. |
| `restricted` | Commercial or proprietary publisher/distributor. Treated as non-redistributable: only metadata (names, identifiers, schedule dates, URLs) is published. |
| `unresolved` | Terms not yet reviewed or unclear. Treated exactly like `restricted`. |
| `project_generated` | Synthetic data created by this repository. |

## How data was acquired

- **Acquisition method.** The January 2024 research slice was built by manual
  transcription from official release pages and PDFs. The repository contains
  no scraper or automated collector (`src/collectors/` is intentionally empty).
- **Retrieval / verification dates.** Audited records carry the audit date
  (`audited_on`) in `config/historical_release_audits/january_2024_source_audit.yaml`.
  Individual release records do not yet store a per-record retrieval date;
  this is a known gap (see README → Limitations).
- **Modifications.** Values are transcribed into a normalized schema
  (typed components, units, UTC and MT5 timestamps). Text components are
  short analytical paraphrases, not verbatim quotations. Where a publisher
  requires disclosure of changes, this paragraph is that disclosure.

## Official sources reviewed as reusable with attribution

### `bls` — U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics

- **Canonical URL:** <https://www.bls.gov/>; release archive pages such as
  <https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_01112024.htm>.
- **Terms:** <https://www.bls.gov/opub/copyright-information.htm> — BLS states
  that its publications are in the public domain (except previously
  copyrighted photographs and illustrations) and asks users to cite BLS.
- **Attribution:** "Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics".
- **Modification notice:** not required; this project discloses its
  restructuring anyway (see "How data was acquired").
- **Repository treatment:** `data/demo/official_releases_allowlist.jsonl`
  contains one reviewed record — the CPI release for December 2023 (published
  2024-01-11 08:30 ET). Values and release time were re-checked against the
  archived release on 2026-09-29.

### `bea` — U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis

- **Canonical URL:** <https://www.bea.gov/>.
- **Terms:** <https://www.bea.gov/help/faq/145> — information on the BEA site
  is in the public domain unless stated otherwise; citation appreciated.
- **Attribution:** "Source: U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis".
- **Repository treatment:** eligible for the allowlist after record-level
  review; no BEA values are published yet.

### `census` — U.S. Census Bureau

- **Canonical URL:** <https://www.census.gov/>.
- **Terms:** <https://www.census.gov/about/policies/citation.html> (citation
  guidance). Works of the U.S. federal government are generally not subject to
  U.S. copyright (17 U.S.C. § 105); the Census Bureau asks to be cited.
- **Attribution:** "Source: U.S. Census Bureau".
- **Repository treatment:** eligible after review; none published yet.

### `federal_reserve_board` — Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System

- **Canonical URL:** <https://www.federalreserve.gov/>.
- **Terms:** <https://www.federalreserve.gov/disclaimer.htm> — unless otherwise
  indicated, information on the Board's website is public domain and may be
  copied and distributed; the Board asks to be cited. Third-party material,
  seals and logos are excluded.
- **Attribution:** "Source: Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System".
- **Repository treatment:** eligible after review; none published yet.

### `eurostat` — Eurostat

- **Canonical URL:** <https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat>.
- **Terms:** <https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/help/copyright-notice> — reuse is
  allowed provided the source is acknowledged and changes are indicated
  (CC BY 4.0 for editorial content; Commission Decision of 12 December 2011 for
  statistical data). Some data (e.g. certain non-EU or third-party data) carry
  additional restrictions.
- **Attribution:** "Source: Eurostat"; modifications must be stated.
- **Repository treatment:** eligible after review; none published yet.

### `ecb` — European Central Bank

- **Canonical URL:** <https://www.ecb.europa.eu/>.
- **Terms:** <https://www.ecb.europa.eu/home/disclaimer/html/index.en.html> —
  reproduction is permitted if the information appears accurately and the ECB
  is cited; modifications (for example calculated growth rates) must be stated
  explicitly. Works with named authors (e.g. working papers) need separate
  permission.
- **Attribution:** "Source: European Central Bank".
- **Repository treatment:** eligible after review; none published yet.

### `destatis` — Statistisches Bundesamt (Destatis)

- **Canonical URL:** <https://www.destatis.de/>.
- **Terms:** <https://www.destatis.de/EN/Service/Legal-Notice/CopyrightGeneral.html>

event definitions (`config/event_rules.yaml`), expected publication dates
(`config/historical_release_audits/january_2024.yaml`), identifiers and URLs.
No values, summaries, notes or copied text are published. Their terms were
not reviewed in detail; the classification is a conservative default, not a
finding that reuse is prohibited.

## Market data

### `mt5_broker_price_data` — MetaTrader 5 price history

- **What:** EUR/USD H1 candles exported by the author from a MetaTrader 5
  terminal (tab-separated MT5 export layout, broker-server timestamps).
- **Status:** restricted — broker data; redistribution rights not established.
- **Repository treatment:** never committed. `generate_eurusd_report.py`
  accepts such files only with `--price-data-origin authentic_private` and
  writes the resulting reports under the git-ignored `data/reports/` (or
  outside the repository).

### `project_synthetic` — synthetic demonstration data

- `data/demo/synthetic_releases.jsonl` — five synthetic release records dated
  January 2030 with placeholder values (`data_origin: synthetic`,
  `synthetic_` ID prefix, "SYNTHETIC" source name, `example.com` URLs).
- `data/demo/SYNTHETIC_eurusd_h1_mt5_format.csv` — synthetic candles starting
  at exactly 1.00000, pure noise with no injected event effect.
- Both are regenerated deterministically by
  `scripts/generate_synthetic_demo_data.py` and are covered by the MIT licence.

## Private data intentionally not included

The author's full January 2024 research catalogue (47 stored releases
covering all 36 expected January publications plus 11 early-February
releases), its Parquet exports and the unredacted source audit mix sources
from every status above. They are kept outside version control. The
`.gitignore` default-denies everything under `data/` except the documented
public demo and placeholder files.

## Enforcement

`python scripts/check_publication_safety.py` lists exactly what an ordinary
`git add .` would publish and fails on: private data locations, raw data
exports, credential files, restricted-source values in public fixtures or the
source audit, undeclared or mislabelled provenance, restricted-source URLs in
application code, and credential-like strings, personal absolute paths or
e-mail addresses in text files. The same checks run in the test suite. They
are an engineering safeguard, not legal verification.
